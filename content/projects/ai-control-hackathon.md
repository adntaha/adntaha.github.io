AI control is the research field of getting useful work out of an AI system you *don't* trust, while limiting the harm it could do if it misbehaved. The AI Control Hackathon ran from June 11 to 18, 2026, with sponsorship from Redwood Research (who invented the field!), NVIDIA, Rootly, and Rubric Labs. I co-organized it with Horizon Omega and HackOS, and we managed to put it together in just under a week.

I introduced the other two organizers to each other, and after some brainstorming, we figured out the hackathon's focus. One small problem: they hadn't met before, and because one of them was about to move away, we had about one week to organize it. I ended up writing most of the [starter repository](https://github.com/hackos-team/hackos-5-quickstart), with setup code for each of the five tracks (incl. commands for a first honest and attack run, an example monitor and protocol to improve on, a policy template for the red team, and a template for new side tasks). During the week I also spent time answering participants' questions, helped fix bugs as they turned up, and coordinated with our contact at Redwood Research.

Naturally, we all coordinated and worked on what needed working. This meant a lot of odd jobs. An example of mine is making the slides that played in the background during the opening ceremony:

![The opening slide: "AI Control Hackathon" over a watercolour Montréal street, with the logos of Horizon Omega, HackOS, Redwood Research, NVIDIA, Rootly and Rubric](/media/ai-control-hackathon/idle-slide.jpg)

In total, 92 people signed up on Luma, 20 teams filled in the team formation form, and 8 of them submitted a project by the end of the week. Fun fact, it was the first event of its kind in Montreal & to be organized independently, and we were contacted by MIT students who were interested in running a similar event on their campus, asking for advice.

Anyhow, a more official organizers' [retrospective](https://horizonomega.substack.com/p/ai-control-hackathon-2026-retrospective) was posted too, and it covers the winning teams and what they built.
